| D-02 | 07 Sep 2026 | Scope | Self-registration, subscription and payment gateway are fully in scope for v1 | The product is sold self-service with no implementation consultant, so registration through to first payroll must succeed with no human at Labourmax involved. |
| D-03 | 07 Sep 2026 | Specification | The table specification is produced at full DDL level | Sheet 02 is buildable directly into Django models and migrations rather than being a discussion document. |
| D-04 | 10 Sep 2026 | Tenancy | One person may hold memberships in several tenants | Supports a bookkeeper or agent serving multiple households. Login gains a tenant picker; app_user.last_tenant_id remembers where they were. |
| D-05 | 10 Sep 2026 | Tenancy | Ownership transfer is an explicit two-sided action requiring re-authentication from both parties | New table tenant_ownership_transfer. The outgoing owner initiates, the incoming admin accepts, the request lapses in 72 hours. A one-click transfer would be a one-click account takeover. |
| D-06 | 10 Sep 2026 | Tenancy | Revoking a membership must not prevent a later membership for the same or a different person in that role | SCHEMA CHANGE: UNIQUE (tenant_id, user_id) becomes a partial unique WHERE revoked_at IS NULL. As originally specified, a revoked user could never be re-added. |
| D-07 | 10 Sep 2026 | Leave | An admin who is also an employee may not approve their own leave; it escalates one level to the owner | Where the owner is themselves the applicant and no other approver exists, they may approve with a mandatory recorded reason, flagged on the record and visible in the leave register. |
| D-08 | 10 Sep 2026 | Access | A terminated employee keeps read access for two months, then it lapses regardless | tenant_membership.access_expires_at set to termination date + 2 months. Payslips must be downloaded inside that window. |
| D-09 | 10 Sep 2026 | Statutory output | The IRP5 is emailed on issue rather than held behind a login | An IRP5 is only available after the tax year closes, which can be more than a year after termination — long after the two-month window shuts. Requires bounce handling that surfaces to the employer. |
| D-10 | 10 Sep 2026 | Identity | Both an email address and a verified mobile number are mandatory, for employee logins as well as employer users | app_user.mobile_number becomes NOT NULL with mobile_verified_at. Safety valve: employee.has_no_email is an honest flag that routes delivery to SMS, so employers do not invent addresses to satisfy a mandatory field. |
| D-11 | 10 Sep 2026 | Security | Payslips are delivered as a secure link, not as an ID-number-protected PDF attachment | An SA ID number has very little entropy — date of birth is widely known, the last digit is a checksum, leaving roughly 20,000 combinations for a known person. It is also the single item POPIA most wants protected. New table secure_document_link; the employee authenticates with a mobile OTP at the portal. |
| D-12 | 10 Sep 2026 | Security | Secure links expire — 90 days for a payslip, 12 months for an IRP5 — and are re-issuable by the employer | A lapsed link must be a self-service re-send, not a support call. |
| D-13 | 10 Sep 2026 | Cost | A successful OTP trusts the device for 30 days | Without it, 200 employees viewing twelve payslips a year is 2,400 SMSes per client per year. With it, roughly one OTP per employee per month. |
| D-14 | 10 Sep 2026 | Compliance | Printing and handing over a payslip remains a first-class delivery channel | BCEA s33 requires the employer to give the employee written particulars of payment. A link satisfies that for a smartphone user and nothing at all for a worker with a feature phone and no data. |
| D-15 | 10 Sep 2026 | Employee list | The employee list groups by pay group rather than by pay basis | A pay group is what a payroll run actually targets, and it carries the basis plus the calendar. Two weekly employees on different week-ending days belong in different runs. |
| D-16 | 10 Sep 2026 | Employee list | Default sort is by first name for domestic employers and by surname for contract cleaning employers, overridable and remembered | Seeded into employer_setting from the sector at onboarding. The same setting drives the attendance grid and the payroll run order — one order everywhere, or the muscle memory is lost. |
| D-17 | 10 Sep 2026 | Employee list | Locale-aware ICU collation on the sort columns, set in the creating migration | Deterministic ICU so LIKE and index-backed search still work; lowercasing handles case. Changing a collation later rewrites the table and every index on it. |
| D-18 | 10 Sep 2026 | Employee list | Denormalised current-state cache on employee: current_pay_group_id and current_pay_basis | Pay basis lives on an effective-dated table, so grouping by it would join and date-filter on every render. Maintained on write AND by a nightly job, because a future-dated change must flip on its effective date with nobody touching the record. |
| D-19 | 10 Sep 2026 | UX | Group headers are suppressed below four employees | Group headings over a two-row list are clutter, and that is the domestic employer's entire experience of the product. |
| D-20 | 12 Sep 2026 | Attendance | The monthly capture grid pre-fills every cell from the employee's work schedule; the employer overrides only the exceptions | This is what makes capture fast. Single-letter codes for day types, keyboard navigation, live bucketing into ordinary, overtime, Sunday and public holiday hours, and a live exception panel that gates approval. Prototype built and reviewed 12 September. |
| D-21 | 12 Sep 2026 | Attendance | Blank and zero are different facts | Blank means nobody has told us and blocks the payroll run; zero means they did not work and does not. Rendered distinctly in the grid. |
| D-22 | 12 Sep 2026 | Attendance | A public holiday not worked and a public holiday worked are separate codes | A public holiday falling on an ordinary working day is paid whether or not it is worked; worked, it attracts double. Two payslip lines from one column, and confusing them is a common way to underpay. |
| D-23 | 07 Sep 2026 | Scope | Employee self-service ships in v1 | Was open at v1.0. Now settled by consequence: the brief requires online leave application by employees, and secure-link payslip delivery requires the portal. |
| D-24 | 12 Sep 2026 | Database | PostgreSQL is the database. MySQL is no longer under consideration | Row-level security as a second isolation wall, exclusion constraints on effective-dated ranges, native range partitioning for the audit and attendance tables, and indexed JSONB for calculation traces. Closes at the first migration: the MySQL equivalents in the column dictionary become reference only. |
| D-25 | 12 Sep 2026 | Attendance | The capture grid does not pre-fill for attendance-driven pay bases — hourly and daily. Salaried bases (weekly, fortnightly, monthly) still pre-fill | An hourly or daily worker is paid strictly off what was captured, so an employer must not be able to approve a month they never looked at. Their days open blank and render as uncaptured, which blocks the payroll run until filled. The dividing line is how the base is paid, not which base it is: if pay follows attendance, the grid never assumes it. Bulk fill from schedule and copy-last-week remain available as explicit employer actions — the difference is that a person chooses them. |
| D-26 | 12 Sep 2026 | Billing | Payment details are captured and verified before the trial starts, under a service agreement created at signup | Framed as a free trial with a 7-day cancellation window inside the agreement, rather than a trial that converts into a contract on day 8. The contract exists from day one, which is cleaner to enforce and easier to defend. Closes O-05. |
| D-27 | 12 Sep 2026 | Billing | Trial dates: cancel-by is signup + 7 days to 23:59:59 SAST, first charge is signup + 8 days | Signup on the 12th means cancel by the 19th and charge on the 20th - deliberately one day more lenient than advertised. Both dates are computed once at acceptance, stored on tenant_service_agreement, and rendered from those stored values on every surface. Nothing recalculates a date. |
| D-28 | 12 Sep 2026 | Billing | The trial-end charge job runs at 09:00 SAST, re-reads subscription status immediately before calling the gateway, and is idempotent on tenant plus charge date | A midnight batch that trusts a status read at queue time is how someone gets charged after cancelling at 23:58. A guard clause in the job itself refuses to charge before first_charge_date. Same-day retries at 13:00 and 17:00, then the dunning ladder. |
| D-29 | 12 Sep 2026 | Billing | The recurring cycle anchors to the lesser of the first-charge day and 28 | A first charge on the 30th means every subsequent month bills on the 28th. Month-end clamping logic would make February behave differently from March. |
| D-30 | 12 Sep 2026 | Billing | The signup disclosure sentence carries the actual selected amount and both exact dates, in plain text immediately above the button | Not in the terms, not a tooltip, not behind a link. Charge terms first, cancellation last. The amount is the real figure for the plan and band selected, so the plan must be chosen before the sentence renders. |
| D-31 | 12 Sep 2026 | Billing | Unpaid accounts are suspended until payment is received, with a payment prompt on login | Suspended means blocked from working - no payroll runs, no employee changes - while existing payslips and IRP5s stay viewable and downloadable, because the employer carries a five-year retention obligation on them. A failed first charge at trial end blocks immediately; a failed renewal for an established customer goes through the dunning ladder first. |
| D-32 | 12 Sep 2026 | Billing | Cancelling during the trial deactivates the payment method and incurs no charge or liability | The gateway token is deleted. Data is kept 30 days with an export offered, then purged - POPIA does not permit holding employee personal information for someone who never became a customer. |
| D-33 | 12 Sep 2026 | Pricing | Domestic standard plan includes 3 active employees, then charges per head | Active means not terminated and not archived. Terminated employees are retained for five years and cost storage, but are not billable. |
| D-34 | 12 Sep 2026 | Pricing | Contract cleaning is banded, and every band above the entry band is a flat base plus a per-head charge for employees above its threshold | Reading A. Charge = base + (headcount - threshold) x per-head rate. This removes the cliff at each boundary: an employer going from 5 cleaners to 6 pays for one extra person, not a whole band's difference. New table plan_price_band; bands are data, so repricing needs no release. |
| D-35 | 12 Sep 2026 | Pricing | Bands are 1-5, 6-10, 11-30, 31-50, 51-100, and over 100 which routes to contact sales | Above a certain size an employer wants a conversation before handing over a card. The top band is flagged requires_contact_sales rather than offering self-service checkout. |
| D-36 | 12 Sep 2026 | Pricing | Contract cleaning signup asks 'How many cleaners do you employ?' as a dropdown that selects the band and prices the disclosure sentence, and pre-fills nothing else | It does not seed employee records, pay groups or anything else. The declared band is stored so the disclosure can be reconstructed, and later compared against actual headcount to see how accurate declarations are. |
| D-37 | 12 Sep 2026 | Pricing | The day-8 charge is the lesser of the disclosed amount and the amount for actual headcount, and may never exceed what the signup sentence said | Understating gets the employer one cheap month, which costs little and removes every argument. Overstating means they are charged the lower actual figure. From the second invoice, actual active headcount governs. |
| D-38 | 12 Sep 2026 | Billing | Bill on active headcount at invoice generation, but record four usage metrics alongside it | Active at invoice, distinct employees paid, payslips finalised, and the high-water mark of active employees during the period. Payroll processed for someone terminated mid-period is real leakage in high-churn contract cleaning, and the high-water mark exposes add-and-remove behaviour around invoice dates without changing the billing rule. Informational at launch; revisit after six months of data. |
| D-39 | 12 Sep 2026 | Billing | Adding an employee beyond the current band prompts at that moment with the new price and an inline adjustment option | Names both prices, states that nothing is charged today, and gives the real next-invoice date. Recorded as a subscription_event with the acknowledging user, which is the evidence if the higher invoice is ever queried. A bulk import evaluates the whole batch and prompts once, after preview. |
| D-40 | 12 Sep 2026 | Billing | Band downgrades apply automatically at the next invoice with a notification, and never prompt | Deliberately asymmetric. An upgrade costs money and needs consent; nobody objects to a smaller bill, so asking for it is friction with no purpose. |
| D-41 | 12 Sep 2026 | Documents | One document table with an exclusive arc, attaching to a tenant, employer, employee or workplace, replacing the earlier employee_document | SCHEMA CHANGE: employee_document is removed; document and document_category replace it. Four nullable owner columns with a CHECK that exactly one is set gives real referential integrity, unlike a generic owner_type/owner_id pair which produces orphans the first time a record is deleted. One table means one upload path, one virus scan, one expiry job, one retention purge and one permission model. |
| D-42 | 12 Sep 2026 | Documents | Workplaces are a document attachment point, so client contracts file against the site | Employees are allocated to a site through employee_position, which makes cost-per-contract reporting possible. Without this, client contracts land on the employer record with the site name typed into the title. |
| D-43 | 12 Sep 2026 | Documents | Document categories are a table, not an enum, carrying the filing rules | What a category attaches to, whether it expires, whether it is required for onboarding, whether the employee may see it, and its default retention. Tenants can add their own without a release. |
| D-44 | 12 Sep 2026 | Documents | Employee self-service shows only the employment contract, payslips and the IRP5 - a whitelist, default deny | document_category.visible_to_employee defaults FALSE and is TRUE for one category. A blacklist would mean a forgotten tick puts a disciplinary letter in the employee's portal. Payslips and IRP5s are not in this table at all - they are reached by secure link. |
| D-45 | 12 Sep 2026 | Documents | CVs are stored for hired employees only; applicant tracking is out of scope | Storing CVs of people who applied and were not hired means holding personal information about people with no employment relationship, with its own consent basis and retention clock. That is a recruitment module, not this one. |
| D-46 | 12 Sep 2026 | Documents | Per-plan storage quota, 20MB upload cap, server-side image downscaling, and an audit row on every download | 500 cleaning tenants at 200 employees and six documents each is over a terabyte. storage.max_mb is a plan_feature limit with a warning at 80%. Every download is audited because these are ID copies and bank letters. |
| D-47 | 12 Sep 2026 | Attendance | Site assignment is a single-site or multi-site option per employee, with no intra-day splitting | Single-site hides the site column from the attendance grid; multi-site exposes a per-day picker. A day belongs to one site. Deliberately small - an allocation table for split days was considered and rejected as disproportionate. |
| D-48 | 12 Sep 2026 | Reporting | Cost per contract reports only what the database already carries. No contract value is stored | Wages, premiums, leave pay, employer UIF and SDL, and the annual bonus accrual shown as its own line because it is a provision rather than a payment. The employer compares against their own client invoice; Labourmax never holds the revenue side. |
| D-49 | 12 Sep 2026 | Statutory maintenance | Statutory rate changes are not ingested automatically. The load is human and verified; the application by effective date is automatic | There is no machine-readable source - SARS and the gazettes publish PDFs. Interpretation is required, as with the 2026 contract cleaning gazette pointing KwaZulu-Natal at a bargaining council agreement rather than giving a number. A wrong rate applied silently across every tenant means reversal runs and corrected IRP5s. |
| D-50 | 12 Sep 2026 | Statutory maintenance | A staleness guard blocks payroll runs beyond the date statutory data is confirmed correct for | reference_data_version.data_current_through. A run whose period ends past it raises a blocking validation issue rather than falling back to superseded rates. The system does not pretend to know something it does not. |
| D-51 | 12 Sep 2026 | Statutory maintenance | The maintenance calendar is held as data in statutory_watch_item, and five recurring reminders were set up outside the product | 15 January season opens, 20 February main load window, 26 February final gate before 1 March, 15 April BCEA threshold, and a quarterly sweep for the irregular items. Each reminder searches the current published figures and reports them against what the system holds, rather than simply prompting. A 'no change' result still advances last_confirmed_date. |
| D-52 | 12 Sep 2026 | Tenancy | A stored file always belongs to exactly one tenant: file_object.tenant_id becomes NOT NULL and the model inherits TenantScopedModel | Found during the P0 migration review: file_object held CVs, ID documents and CIPC documents with a nullable tenant_id, no scoped manager and no RLS policy, and the isolation suite did not discover it. Platform-owned assets (sector document templates, announcement attachments) get their own table in P9 rather than a nullable hole in the table holding customer identity documents. |
| D-53 | 12 Sep 2026 | Tenancy | Security and operations tables with a legitimately nullable tenant_id use a second explicit base, TenantOptionalModel, rather than no protection at all | Applies to otp_challenge, login_audit, audit_log and background_job - an OTP issued before a tenant is picked, a login attempt that failed before we knew who it was. Rules: a tenant session sees only its own rows and never the NULL-tenant ones; a session with no tenant pinned sees the NULL-tenant rows only; the platform console sees everything and only inside platform_context(). AMENDED same day: the third rule was originally 'writable but not readable' for audit_log and background_job. That is not implementable - Django appends RETURNING id to every INSERT and PostgreSQL applies the policy's USING clause to rows returned that way, so a row the USING clause rejects cannot be inserted at all. Given up: a secondary defence against an unauthenticated request reading platform audit rows. Kept: no tenant can ever see another tenant's rows, or the platform's. |
| D-54 | 12 Sep 2026 | Security | Cross-tenant visibility is granted by one named function, platform_context(), which lifts the manager filter and the RLS policy together and logs every entry | The alternative was a flag threaded through call sites, which is unreviewable. One deliberate, greppable, logged hole beats five silent ones. A request never gets platform access - only the superuser console and named maintenance jobs enter it. |
| D-55 | 12 Sep 2026 | Data integrity | Append-only tables are enforced by a database trigger, not by REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE FROM PUBLIC | The revoke does nothing: a table's owner holds its privileges implicitly and the application connects as the owner. Identical trap to row-level security without FORCE - the protection reads convincingly and is absent. The POPIA retention purge (P11) is the one legitimate deleter and sets labourmax.allow_ledger_maintenance for its duration, in the same style as platform_context(). Applied to audit_log at P0; leave_transaction, payslip and payslip_line follow in P6 and P7. |
| D-56 | 13 Sep 2026 | Statutory maintenance | Reference data is loaded from a JSON fixture through one loader that refuses the whole file on any problem, and never updates an existing row | All or nothing in one transaction; no citation, no load; a fixture row matching an existing natural key with a different value is refused rather than applied, because a statutory value is superseded by a new effective-dated row and never edited. Foreign keys are natural keys - 'DOMESTIC', never 7 - so a fixture is portable between development, CI and production. The fixture is fingerprinted with SHA-256 onto reference_data_version.checksum, which makes a quietly edited file detectable and an identical re-run a no-op. |
| D-57 | 13 Sep 2026 | Statutory maintenance | Closing a superseded period is declared by the fixture, never inferred by the loader | An annual load collides with last year's open-ended row, and the fix is a modification of already-verified data. One top-level key, closes_open_periods_from, states it; every row closed is listed in the load report for the verifier to check against the source document. Without the declaration the exclusion constraint refuses the load, which is the correct outcome. |
| D-58 | 13 Sep 2026 | Statutory data | A calculator never queries a statutory table. Every effective-date lookup goes through statutory.resolve, and missing data raises rather than returning None | Written by hand in each calculator the query eventually appears as effective_to__gte, which is off by one day, in one place, and produces a payslip that is wrong only for employees paid on the day a rate changed. Missing data raises because a calculator handed None for the UIF ceiling still produces a payslip. Narrowing falls back in a fixed documented order - grade to area to sector to the National Minimum Wage, which genuinely is the floor - and widening never does. |
| D-59 | 13 Sep 2026 | Statutory data | The no-hard-coded-rate rule is a test, not a grep | P2's definition of done ran as a person's grep on the day the phase closed. It now runs on every commit: no Decimal constant outside a very short allowlist and no float literal anywhere in application code, no ORM or clock in calculators/, and no database default on any rule set column. Stated in terms of shape rather than of particular figures, so it keeps working for rates nobody has loaded yet. |
| D-60 | 13 Sep 2026 | Statutory data | PAYE bands are stored with income_from set to the 'taxable income above R' figure SARS prints, not the band's printed lower bound | SARS prints band 2 as starting at R245 101. Stored that way, a taxable income of R245 100,50 falls between two bands and resolves to nothing, mid payroll run. Stored as R245 100 the bands touch, and the two readings agree at the touch point by construction - 18% of R245 100 is R44 118, which is band 2's base. Recorded in the notes column of every affected row. |
| D-61 | 13 Sep 2026 | Statutory data | Contract cleaning areas are A (metros), B (named local councils) and C (KwaZulu-Natal), per the gazette | The model's own help text had B and C the other way round, with KwaZulu-Natal as Area B. GN R.7083 GG 54075 of 3 February 2026 says otherwise. Codes are AREA_A, AREA_B, AREA_C_KZN. Found by reading the gazette rather than the earlier draft - which is the argument for researching primary sources rather than converting the workbook. |
| D-62 | 13 Sep 2026 | Statutory maintenance | Verification is gated on the data reconciling with itself, checked by machine before a person signs it off | statutory/checks.py: every PAYE base must equal the tax on the bands below it, every tax threshold must equal its cumulative rebates divided by the lowest rate, bands must touch with no gap, and no citation may be a placeholder like 'TBC'. Published statutory tables are internally redundant, so one mistyped digit stops the ladder reconciling. manage.py checkstatutory runs it; verifystatutory refuses while anything blocking is outstanding. It proves the figures agree with each other, never that they are the right figures - that is the golden tests' job in P7. |
| D-63 | 13 Sep 2026 | Statutory data | Where a figure cannot be found in a primary source it is absent from the fixture, not estimated | Contract cleaning Area C carries no rate: the gazette gives KwaZulu-Natal no figure and points at the BCCCI collective agreement. Rather than interpolate, the area is loaded with uses_bargaining_council_rates set, the watch list carries it as change_published_not_loaded, and a KwaZulu-Natal contract cleaning employer cannot be onboarded until the agreement is in hand. Two further figures - the UIF ceiling's gazette number and the COIDA earnings ceiling - are loaded from secondary sources and carry a VERIFY note in their notes column. |
| D-64 | 13 Sep 2026 | Statutory data | Parental leave is stored as months plus days, the maternity restriction is stored as two separate columns, and annual_bonus_month is nullable. THE WORKBOOK NEEDS THE SAME THREE CORRECTIONS | Departures from sheet 02, each found by reading the source. (1) Van Wyk v Minister of Employment and Labour [2025] ZACC 20 gives all parents four months and ten days - four calendar months from January is not the same number of days as four from June, so a single day count would bake one interpretation into the reference data. (2) The column was maternity_pre_birth_reserved_days; the statute's hard rule is the other way round - a birth mother may not work for six weeks AFTER the birth unless certified fit, and separately may start up to four weeks before. Two rules, two columns, and the post-birth one is the one with teeth. (3) annual_bonus_month was NOT NULL with a 1-12 CHECK, so the BCEA default and the domestic sector - neither of which has a statutory bonus - would have had to name a month they never pay in. |
| D-65 | 13 Sep 2026 | Statutory data | A rule set column with no statutory figure behind it is loaded as zero with a note saying the figure does not exist, never as a plausible number | Four columns: night_allowance_value on both rows (BCEA s17(2) requires an allowance and deliberately sets no amount - the employer does), and the three standby columns plus the accommodation cap on the BCEA row (neither concept is in the Act; both are SD7's). The notes column carries 'ZERO MEANS THERE IS NO STATUTORY FIGURE' in capitals, because a calculator author reading 0.0000 will otherwise pay nothing for night work. |
| D-66 | 13 Sep 2026 | Statutory data | Contract cleaning gets no rule set until SD1 has been read, and falls back to the BCEA default in the meantime | A contract cleaning rule set that copied the BCEA values under a citation naming SD1 would hide the gap; the fallback shows it. sector.has_statutory_annual_bonus is True for contract cleaning while no rule set carries the bonus terms, and a test asserts that contradiction stays visible until SD1 is loaded. |
